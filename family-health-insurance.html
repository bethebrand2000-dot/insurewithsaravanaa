Family floater health insurance

One policy for spouse, children and sometimes parents, sized for a bad year and not just an average one.

How a family floater works

A floater gives your whole family one shared sum insured. It is often more economical than separate policies, since you pay a single premium.

When a floater is wise, and when it is not

Floaters suit young families with similar needs. Parents or elders often do better with their own policy, because their age and health history affect price and waiting periods. We look at your family as it is.

Sizing the cover

If two people need treatment in the same year, the shared sum insured can fall quickly. I help you think about the worst year, and whether the plan can be topped up.

What I check with you

  • Who should be on the same policy
  • Whether parents need a separate plan
  • How the sum insured is shared
  • Maternity and newborn terms
  • What happens when children grow up

Common questions

Should my parents be on my family floater?

Usually it is better for parents to have a separate policy. Their age and health history can raise the premium for everyone on a shared plan.

Can I add a new family member later?

Often yes at renewal, subject to the plan. Ask me about the rules before you buy.
