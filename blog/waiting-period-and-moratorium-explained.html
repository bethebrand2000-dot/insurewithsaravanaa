Waiting periods and the moratorium, explained simply

By Rtn. S. Saravanakumar · 9 November 2026 · 4 min read

Most people only discover these terms at claim time. Understanding them at the start can save a family a great deal of stress.

The initial waiting period

Most policies do not cover illness for a short period after you buy, commonly 30 days. Accidents are usually treated differently. Check your policy wording for the exact period.

Waiting for pre-existing conditions

If you already have a condition such as diabetes or high blood pressure, the policy may not cover treatment for it until a waiting period has passed. IRDAI's 2024 master circular on health insurance limited this waiting period to a maximum of three years. Many plans are shorter, so always confirm.

Waiting for specific illnesses

Some treatments, such as certain surgeries, have their own waiting periods, again within the limit set by the regulator.

The moratorium period

After you have held a health policy continuously for five years (60 months), the insurer generally cannot contest a claim on grounds such as non-disclosure, except for proven fraud and permanent exclusions in the policy. IRDAI shortened this from eight years to five in 2024.

Why continuity matters: a break in your policy can restart these clocks. Renew on time, and ask before switching plans.

Both periods run only if you answered every question honestly on the proposal form. That is the single most important thing you can do to protect your future claim.
